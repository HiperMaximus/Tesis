\textbf{Title:} Semi-supervised model of equivariant convolutional neural networks for segmentation and classification of histopathological images.\footnote{Bachelor thesis.}

\textbf{Author:} \Author.\footnote{Faculty of Physical-Mechanical Engineering. School of Systems Engineering and Informatics. Advisor: \DirectorName, \DirectorDegree}

\textbf{Keywords:} equivariant convolutional neural networks, histopathological images, digital image processing, semi-supervised learning.

\textbf{Description:}

\begingroup\small

Digital pathology enables the preservation and computational analysis of
histopathological images, whose morphological information is essential for
characterizing tissues and studying diseases such as cancer. Developing models for this
analysis depends on annotations that require pathologists' time and specialized
knowledge. Their limited availability motivates semi-supervised learning, which uses
unlabeled regions to learn representations subsequently used in supervised tasks. At the
microscopic scale, the same cellular structure can appear in different orientations.
Exploiting this symmetry through parameter sharing allows features common to those
orientations to be learned with fewer parameters, motivating the study of its usefulness
when annotations are scarce. This study investigated how equivariance to continuous
rotations affects reconstruction, predictive performance, and the organization of a
variational autoencoder's latent space.

Two residual variational autoencoders (VAEs) were compared: a baseline VAE with
conventional convolutions and an \(\mathrm{SO}(2)\)-equivariant VAE with steerable convolutions.
Both shared the overall structure and were trained on the same 300,000 patches from
UBC-OCEAN. The equivariant model used 70.2\% fewer learned parameters and had a higher
estimated convolutional cost.

The 513 whole-slide images (WSIs) were divided into disjoint populations for
unsupervised development, supervised development, and testing. After VAE training, their
parameters were frozen, and classifiers with the same architecture were trained on the
posterior mean. The downstream tasks comprised diagnosis of five ovarian carcinoma
subtypes through multiple-instance learning and segmentation of tumor, stroma, and
necrosis on a patch grid. Segmentation was evaluated in annotated regions of high
purity, using five per-class label budgets. One initialization was evaluated per model
and classifier. The shared test set included 23 WSIs; confidence intervals were
estimated through paired resampling at the slide level.

\newpage

The baseline VAE achieved a mean absolute error of 0.0629, compared with 0.0642 for the
equivariant model. Diagnostic macro-F1 scores were 0.389 and 0.483, respectively. The
95\% intervals for both differences included zero. In segmentation, the equivariant
representation achieved higher macro-F1 under four of the five label budgets; the
simultaneous interval excluded zero only at 500 labels per class. When all available
labels were used, the point estimate favored the baseline VAE.

The latent-space analysis used 25 fixed validation patches and distinguished three
tests: rotating and decoding the latent representation; encoding a rotated image,
undoing the rotation in the latent representation, and decoding it; and reconstructing
a rotated image using the encoder and decoder together. For exact quarter-turn
rotations, the median error ratio when rotating and decoding the latent representation
was 0.709 for the baseline VAE and \(2{.}18\times10^{-6}\) for the
\(\mathrm{SO}(2)\)-equivariant VAE.
Interpolated rotations also yielded lower errors in the first two tests for this model,
whereas the comparison of the complete autoencoder under continuous rotations favored
the baseline VAE. Projections from principal component analysis (PCA)
and local geometry measures complemented this characterization. The clearest
finding was a more consistent rotational response when transforming and decoding the
latent representation. Predictive performance varied with the task and the number of
labels.
